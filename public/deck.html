Slide 03 · Regulatory Catalyst

Section 176(3) BNSS: The Mandatory Scene Visit

THE LAW: SECTION 176(3) BNSS 2023

"The officer in charge shall cause a forensic expert to visit the crime scene... and cause videography of the scene on mobile phone or other electronic device."

In force across India since 1 July 2024.
⚖️ Not Discretionary Any More
Mandatory for all offences with imprisonment of 7 years or more (Homicide, Rape, Armed Dacoity, Serious Arson).
📱 The Mobile Phone Clause
The statute specifically contemplates mobile phone capture—providing statutory backing for remote supervision.

Slide 05 · The Solution

Three Pieces That Let One Expert Supervise Ten Scenes

📱

01 · Guided Capture

The investigating officer opens the DFSS offence checklist, films on their phone, and the expert watches and directs live via coordinate markers.

➔ Replaces the 4-hour drive to the scene.
⛓️

02 · Chain of Custody

Every exhibit logged at capture with GPS, timestamp, and SHA-256 hash. Follows Section 63 BSA 2023 electronic evidence rules.

➔ Replaces the paper register & loose bags.
🧪

03 · Lab Triage

The FSL sees incoming cases with priority and type before dispatch. DNA and Ballistics batch together; pendency becomes actionable.

➔ Replaces the invisible queue.

Slide 09 · The Moat

The Moat is the Evidence Chain, Not the Camera App

"Why not a generic app developer?"

Anyone can film a scene on an iPhone or Android.
Not anyone can prove the file was never edited in a murder trial.

The value is the auditability of the record under Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023, Section 63, and the district court's trust in it.
1. Unbroken BSA §63 Ledger:
Cryptographic SHA-256 chains render tampering impossible to conceal from the magistrate.
2. Institutional Stickiness:
Once a district's malkhana and regional FSL rely on Pramaan, switching vendors breaks historical continuity.
3. Three Years of Case History:
Every solved trial deepens the institutional moat against new entrants.

Slide 11 · Risk Mitigation

Four Fatal Risks & The First Move On Each

Risk 1: Courts Reject Remote Capture

Defence argues the expert was not physically present.

First Move: Test with a magistrate and defence counsel before writing production code.
Risk 2: Government Builds It In-House

e-Forensics platform already exists in central labs.

First Move: Sell the mobile capture layer e-Forensics lacks; integrate rather than replace.
Risk 3: Slow State Procurement (Tenders take 2 years)

State-level tenders take 18–24 months to clear.

First Move: Price at ₹12 Lakh to sit within discretionary pilot budgets, avoiding tenders.
Risk 4: Officers Refuse to Use It

Scene teams are overworked and tech-resistant.

First Move: Design for one-handed use at 2 AM; measure usable court files, not logins.
